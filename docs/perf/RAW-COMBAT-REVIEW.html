RAW COMBAT REVIEW

Why the phone runs hot even with Particles + Shadows/Blur OFF — from two on-device logs (iPhone, iOS 18.7, WKWebView, DPR 3, 4 cores, rAF capped at 60 fps).

Heat ≠ FPS. With particles and shadows off, the game holds the 60 fps cap (frame time median 17ms) — but the phone still cooks. That's because heat is sustained work that never lets the SoC idle, not dropped frames. The proof is in the logs: particles 0 · impacts 0 · trails 0, yet anim (running CSS animations + WAAPI) never falls below ~40–60 and spikes to ~150 for the whole session. Every one of those is the GPU compositor doing work on every display refresh, forever. Turning off particles/shadows cut GPU fill (which helped frame-time) but did nothing to this animation/CPU floor — which is exactly why FPS got better and heat did not.

The heat sources, ranked

1Always-on animation: … infinite — the idle heat floor CRITICAL

Dozens of CSS keyframe animations run continuously on the combat + merge screens. They are NOT tied to combat events — they loop forever at the display refresh, so the compositor + main thread never idle, on every screen, whether or not anything is happening. This is the single biggest thermal driver and it's completely independent of the particle/shadow toggles. It matches the ~40–60 anim floor the logs show even when combat quietens.

Fix: this is ~30–60 permanent compositor animations. Biggest thermal win in the whole game: (a) drop the constant hero bob / enemy sway (the two highest-count, always-on ones) or run them far slower / pause them when not the active screen; (b) pause ambient loops (embers, generator cogs, merge hints) via animation-play-state: paused when idle or off-screen; (c) only animate limit sweep/pips on the hero(es) that actually changed. Feel cost: less "alive" idle motion — but this is the lever that most reduces steady-state heat/battery.

2WAAPI per-hit combat animations HIGH

On top of the ambient floor, active fighting adds element.animate() calls that push anim to 100–150: the per-hit chip flash (2 animations/hit), attacker telegraphs, and the floating damage numbers (a fresh <div> + .animate() each, then removed). These scale with hit rate and drive both the compositor and DOM churn (nodes climb 450→640).

3Several always-on requestAnimationFrame loops HIGH

Multiple rAF loops each wake the CPU every frame (60×/s) whether or not there's visible change: the sim game-loop.ts, the shared bar-ticker (HP/limit bar easing — active the whole fight), and the fx-engine loop, plus per-screen loops in Board/Orders/Autobattler. Even the sim loop fires 60×/s to advance a 5 Hz simulation. Constant rAF wakeups = the CPU never sleeps = heat.

Fix: ensure every loop parks when there's nothing to animate (the fx-engine already does; verify bar-ticker stops when all bars are settled). Bigger lever below (#4). Feel cost: none if parking is correct.

4Rendering a 5 Hz sim at 60 fps MED — biggest single knob

The battle simulation steps at tickMs = 200 (5 Hz), but the whole view + canvas + animations render at the 60 fps cap — ~12× more often than the sim changes. On a 4-core phone that's a lot of redundant compositor/CPU work per second purely for smoothness.

Fix: cap combat rendering to ~30 fps (throttle the rAF-driven visuals / bar-ticker to every other frame). Roughly halves steady-state per-frame work → a large, broad heat reduction. Feel cost: bars/idle motion at 30fps instead of 60 — usually imperceptible for an idle auto-battler.

5will-change layers + DOM churn MED

Up to 21 will-change nodes = 21 promoted GPU compositor layers held in memory; plus persistent will-change: transform on .mb-tile / .mb-shaker / chip arts. Combined with the damage-number create→animate→remove churn (DOM 450→640), this is steady GPU-layer management + style recalc + GC.

Fix: drop will-change from elements that aren't mid-transition (set it only during an animation, clear after); pool damage-number nodes instead of create/remove. Feel cost: none.

Why the Particles / Shadows toggles didn't cool it

Those toggles removed GPU fill + compositing cost (canvas particles, drop-shadow/blur passes) — which is a frame-time cost, so FPS improved and the worst hitches shrank. But heat is dominated by work that happens every frame regardless of fill: the ~40–60 always-on animations (#1), the rAF wakeups (#3), the 60fps-over-5Hz render (#4), and React reconciling the combat surface each tick. None of those are touched by the particle/shadow flags — so the SoC stays pinned and the phone stays hot.
